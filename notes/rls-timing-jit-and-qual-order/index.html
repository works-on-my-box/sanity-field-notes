Timing an RLS policy: JIT and qual order change the number several-fold

2026-09-29 · PostgreSQL#rls

Symptom

count(*) under an EXISTS policy took 441 ms on the first measurement and 19 ms on the third; a per-row auth.uid() variant took 133 ms in one run and 17 ms in another for the same query; on a machine where two subagents were working in parallel the JIT section doubled (216 ms vs 125 ms).

Cause

The cost estimate of the correlated EXISTS in the policy was 3.2 million (the planner prices the per-row subplan alternative and executes the hashed one), triggering about 125 ms of JIT compilation on every query; in the second case the cheap hash subplan came first in the AND, so auth.uid() was evaluated only for surviving rows.

Rule

First explain (analyze, costs on) and the JIT section, then believe the milliseconds; for policy measurements set jit = off explicitly and show the trap separately; measure the expression in the position the claim is about (inside the policy), not in a WHERE next to another policy; take the final run on a quiet machine after the subagents are done.
